   verbatim from arXiv:1610.01808 p.3): `D_ε(ρ) = (1-ε)ρ + ε·I/2` -- "with probability 1-ε, keep
   the state; with probability ε, discard it and replace with the maximally mixed state."
3. The structural resemblance AND the structural gap between depolarizing and this project's own
   photon loss: both are "discard the qubit's information" events, but depolarizing *replaces*
   with a randomized guess that still gets measured/fed forward, while this project's convention
   *discards* a lost photon's outcome into `residual`/`herald_failure_prob` and excludes it from
   `dist` entirely (`18-RESEARCH.md` Finding 3's explicit framing of this gap).
4. Three genuinely different, physically-motivated candidate directions for the η→ε mapping,
   presented as OPTIONS, not a recommendation (`18-RESEARCH.md`'s own "Guidance for the HARD-04
   Attempt-First Checkpoint" section, reproduced here without picking one):
   (a) **Erasure-as-depolarizing**: `ε ≈ 1-η` directly (weight-1), with the open question of
       whether the discard-vs-guess distinction (ingredient 3) needs reconciling first.
   (b) **Compounded gate-failure rate**: derive `ε` from how much the herald-conditioned output
       distribution (measured in `results/phase18_mixed_loss_sweep.csv`) deviates from the
       lossless herald-conditioned distribution as η varies, rather than from raw transmittance
       alone.
   (c) **Fitted effective channel**: compute the real induced single-qubit channel (tracing out
       everything but one qubit's mode-pair) and find the depolarizing rate ε whose D_ε is closest
       (diamond-norm or trace-distance) to it -- the most rigorous, also the heaviest option.
5. The goal: derive a stated, defensible η→ε translation (picking one of the three directions
   above, or a genuine hybrid, with explicit assumptions), then use it to state plainly which
   regime(s) this project's tested η range (`hardness.sweep.ETA_GRID`'s actual 7 values) sits in
   relative to (i) Aaronson-Brod's fixed-loss-count regime (arXiv:1510.05245, Plan 18-01's fresh
   read) and (ii) BMS's depolarizing-threshold regime (arXiv:1610.01808 Theorem 4 -- "an
   arbitrarily small constant ε destroys hardness for anticoncentrated circuits," no single
   numeric threshold, per `18-RESEARCH.md` Finding 3's precise-statement caveat).
