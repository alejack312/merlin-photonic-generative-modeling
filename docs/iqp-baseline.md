## IQP Circuit Structure & Hardness

- **Circuit recipe.** Every qubit starts in the `|+⟩` state (Hadamard on `|0⟩`). A middle layer of gates diagonal in the Z-basis is applied (in the reference implementation, `MultiRZ(2θⱼ, wires=gⱼ)` for each generator `gⱼ`). Hadamards are applied again and the circuit is measured in the computational basis; equivalently, measurement lands in the Hadamard-conjugate (X) basis relative to the diagonal gate layer.
- **"Instantaneous" means no fixed gate order.** Every gate in the middle layer is diagonal in the same basis, so they all commute pairwise. There is no operator-ordering to track: this is where the "I" in IQP comes from, and it is the structural property the classical-training trick below depends on.
- **The classical-training trick (Van den Nest's cosine formula).** Any Z-word expectation value under the circuit has a closed form:

  ```
  ⟨Z_a⟩ = E_{z ~ Uniform({0,1}^n)} [ cos(Φ(θ, z, a)) ]
  Φ(θ, z, a) = Σ_j (a·g_j mod 2) · 2θ_j · (-1)^(z·g_j)
  ```

  This turns "compute an expectation value" into averaging a cosine over randomly sampled classical bitstrings `z`: plain integer/float arithmetic, no `2^n`-dimensional state vector, no matrix exponentiation. Commutativity of the gate layer is exactly what makes this closed form exist: because there's no ordering ambiguity, the output distribution has tractable Fourier/Walsh structure over Z-word expectations.
- **Training is classically tractable; sampling is believed hard.** The cosine-formula trick only ever produces expectation values, never full samples. Actually drawing a bitstring from the circuit's real output distribution still requires running the quantum circuit (or a full state-vector simulator), believed classically hard, which is the entire basis for a quantum-advantage claim here. Training and sampling are different computational tasks with very different classical tractability, and it's easy to conflate them.
  - **Precise hardness statement (verified against Bremner, Montanaro & Shepherd, arXiv:1504.07999, Theorem 1, p.1):** *conditional* on either of two named average-case hardness conjectures (Ising partition-function estimation, or random-degree-3-polynomial zero-counting over 𝔽₂; both conjectures' *worst-case* analogues are proven, Proposition 8), no classical poly-time algorithm can sample from an IQP circuit's output distribution to within **1/192 ℓ1-error**, or the Polynomial Hierarchy collapses to its third level. "Believed hard" above should be read as "conditionally hard, pending an open average-case lift of a proven worst-case result", not unconditional.
  - **Correction:** the "Van den Nest cosine formula" above is a *different* paper's contribution: Bremner/Montanaro/Shepherd 2015 does not contain or derive it, and doesn't discuss classical-training tractability at all. The two claims (cosine-formula training trick; sampling-hardness conjecture) should not be read as coming from the same source.
  - **Attribution independently confirmed 2026-08-17** (closing a gap flagged by a Phase 18 code review; the correction above removed the wrong citation but never verified the right one): Recio-Armengol, Ahmed & Bowles (arXiv:2503.02934v2, already in this repo's `docs/papers/`) state and prove this exact formula as their **Proposition 1 ("den Nest, 2010")**, Eq. 13: `⟨Z_a⟩ = E_{z~U}[cos(Σ_j θ_j(-1)^(g_j·z)(1-(-1)^(g_j·a)))]`, matching this doc's formula above up to notation. This confirms the "Van den Nest" attribution is correct, sourced via an independent secondary confirmation that itself derives and cites the result (not a bare unverified claim). **Residual gap, stated plainly:** the primary den Nest 2010 paper itself has not been independently read by this project. Two candidate papers exist under that author/year (den Nest's Gottesman-Knill-theorem paper, arXiv:0811.0898, and "Simulating quantum computers with probabilistic methods," arXiv:0911.1624, journal-published 2011 as *Quantum Information & Computation* 11); the latter is the better topical match (probabilistic/Monte-Carlo classical simulation methods) but this was not confirmed by reading it directly.
- **Same structural skeleton Douce et al. ports to CV.** The H-diagonal-gates-H sandwich (`|+⟩` prep → commuting diagonal layer → Hadamard-conjugate measurement) is exactly the DV starting point `docs/iqp-lit-scoping.md` describes Douce et al. (2017) building its continuous-quadrature analogue from. ## Barren-Plateau Trainability

- **Formal definition.** A barren plateau is the regime where the loss-gradient variance decays exponentially in qubit count: `Var_{θ~D}[∂_θᵢ L] = O(2^{-αn})` for some `α > 0`. When this holds, distinguishing a useful descent direction from noise requires an exponential number of measurement shots, and training does not scale.
- **The open question this baseline investigated.** Standard results for generic random circuits (unitary 2-designs) say barren plateaus are the rule as circuit depth/qubit count grow. IQP is *not* a 2-design: its gate set is far more structured (all diagonal, all commuting). Does that structure protect trainability, or is exponential gradient decay unavoidable regardless?
- **Average-case framing matters.** Barren-plateau results are statements about the *average* gradient over the parameter landscape, not pointwise guarantees (Larocca et al., "A Review of Barren Plateaus in Variational Quantum Computing," arXiv:2405.00781v2), so "trainable valleys"/"fertile valleys"/"narrow gorges" can exist even where the ensemble average looks flat. This is why initialization scheme (e.g. small-angle, θ near 0) is a live variable rather than a footnote.
  - **Verified 2026-08-12:** this attribution is accurate as stated: the review makes this exact argument verbatim in two places (p.4, Sec III.A and p.14, Sec XI outlook). Its formal BP definition is `Var[∂L] ∈ O(1/b^n)` for generic base `b>1` (Eq. 6); the doc's `2^-αn` phrasing is an equivalent paraphrase (`α = log₂(b)`), not the paper's literal notation.
  - **IQP is never named in this review, or in the general VQA review checked (Cerezo et al., "Variational Quantum Algorithms," arXiv:2012.09265v2).** The closest structural analogs discussed are commuting-gate circuits (ref [164] in Larocca et al., "a PQC where all the gates commute... the size of the algebra is exactly equal to the number of distinct gate generators", provably small dynamical Lie algebra, hence BP-resistant), matchgate/Ising-HVA circuits, and linear-optics continuous-variable PQCs on coherent light (also provably small DLA). "Does IQP's specific structure protect trainability" is a genuinely open question in the mainstream BP literature, not one with a direct answer to cite, worth stating plainly in any write-up rather than implying the literature has already settled it.
- **Empirical resolution: structure alone does not predict plateaus.** A purely structural predicate (`bounded_degree AND high_overlap`) had 0% recall against observed plateaus: structure alone told you almost nothing. The rule that actually worked combines initialization, system size, and one structural escape hatch:

  ```
  plateau if small_angle
  or plateau if uniform and n >= 6 and not complete_graph_like
  ```

  Scored against 283 training + holdout rows: 97.9% accuracy, 100% precision, 97.5% recall (0 false positives, 6 false negatives, all in an unresolved `uniform × n=4` corner). The `not complete_graph_like` clause is load-bearing: removing it reintroduces false positives on complete-graph structures.
- **Anti-concentration alone is an insufficient success criterion.** A learned distribution can pass a naive anti-concentration check and still have small MMD loss while being far more concentrated than its target: every exact-`n=9` sweep found the learned distribution smoother/more concentrated than target regardless of bandwidth. Marginal-agreement diagnostics (does the learned distribution match the target's actual correlational structure) are the more informative check than anti-concentration in isolation.

## Fresh Primary-Source Verification (2026-08-12)

Direct read of all 9 PDFs (8 distinct papers; one duplicate arXiv ID), originally read from `C:\Users\cuqui\iqp-mmd-barren-plateau\docs\papers` and now also available locally at [`docs/papers/`](papers/) in this repo, done because the original compilation above relied on a sibling project's secondhand vault notes rather than the papers themselves. Corrections are inlined above; genuinely new findings, not previously reflected anywhere in this doc, are below.

- **MMD's trainability depends on kernel bandwidth, not just circuit structure or init (Rudolph et al., "Trainability barriers and opportunities in quantum generative modeling," arXiv:2305.02881v2).** For a bitstring-Hamming-distance Gaussian kernel, MMD² decomposes into a weighted sum of Pauli-Z-string observables whose average "bodyness" is controlled by the bandwidth `σ`: a **constant** (`O(1)`, n-independent) bandwidth makes the observable global and `Var[MMD] ∈ O(1/b^n)`: exponentially concentrated, untrainable, *regardless of ansatz or initialization*; a bandwidth scaled **linearly with system size** (`σ ∈ Θ(n)`) keeps the observable predominantly low-bodied and `Var[MMD] ∈ Ω(1/n)`. **This project's Phase 17 gradient-variance sweep used a fixed `SIGMA = 0.1` across all tested n (`trainability/sweep.py:29`, inherited unchanged from v1.0's own fixed-bandwidth convention)**, exactly the regime this paper's Theorem 2 identifies as independently sufficient to cause exponential decay, on top of whatever effect circuit structure or initialization scheme (uniform vs. small_angle) contributes. The paper's kernel operates on bitstring Hamming distance; this project's kernel operates on Euclidean distance between continuous spatial bin-centers, so the bodyness decomposition doesn't translate mechanically, but the qualitative risk (fixed bandwidth confounding the init/structure comparison) is a live methodological question for Phase 17's already-shipped result, not resolved by this doc alone. **Flagged for the owner's interpretation, not resolved here.**
- **Small-angle/identity initialization is not a general trainability guarantee (Mhiri et al., "A unifying account of warm start guarantees for patches of quantum landscapes," arXiv:2502.07889v1).** The paper proves gradients/curvature near a point cannot vanish exponentially *if* that point has non-exponentially-small curvature to begin with, but explicitly shows (Appendix H) a concrete circuit where curvature at θ=0 is exactly zero by construction, calling small-angle guarantees "not general." Structured/commuting circuits are flagged as the specific risk case: "an extreme example... is one that completely commutes with the observable or state. In that case, the cost function is fixed for all θ values and its variance trivially becomes zero" (p.5-6): directly relevant to an IQP-style circuit built from commuting diagonal gates. This gives a theoretically-grounded, citable reason Phase 17's `small_angle` results came out inconclusive rather than a clean call either way (the paper's own theory has an acknowledged gap between its proven polynomial-decay and exponential-decay regimes).
- **A near-identical literature model exists and analytically confirms this project's own uniform-init finding (Recio-Armengol, Ahmed & Bowles, "Train on classical, deploy on quantum: scaling generative quantum machine learning to a thousand qubits," arXiv:2503.02934v2).** Trains parameterized IQP circuits (weight-1/weight-2+ generators, Hadamard-dual to this project's Z/ZZ convention) up to n=1000 with the same MMD² loss family. Their own Sec. 9.3 *analytically derives* exponential concentration of single-qubit-type observables under random/uniform initialization (`⟨Z₁⟩ = ∏cos(2θₖ)` over n terms), the same signature this project measured empirically. Their fix is not a claim that the circuit class is inherently BP-free; it's a specific **data-dependent initialization** (weight-1 angles set to `arcsin(√⟨x_j⟩)` from empirical single-bit training-data means; weight-2 angles set proportional to empirical pairwise covariances): a concrete, portable alternative to a fixed-magnitude "small_angle" scheme, directly testable at this project's own n=5-6 scale. They candidly note they could not statistically rule out that their own random-init gradients were simply too small to detect, rather than provably non-vanishing, so this is evidence for a specific alternative worth trying, not proof that it would work here.
- **Trainability and sampling-hardness may be two faces of the same anticoncentration property, not independent findings (Herbst, Brandić & Pérez-Salinas, "Limits of quantum generative models with classical sampling hardness," arXiv:2512.24801v1).** Formal result: circuits whose output distributions anticoncentrate (the same property IQP's hardness proof depends on) cause any MMD-type loss to concentrate exponentially, i.e., anticoncentration-driven hardness and anticoncentration-driven untrainability are predicted to co-occur, not trade off unpredictably. Their own numerics include IQP directly (via PennyLane/IQPopt) and show it concentrating faster than comparable circuit families. Consequence for this project: Phase 17's already-measured exponential-decay trainability result and Phase 18's upcoming hardness-under-loss result are predicted by this framework to be **consistent, not contradictory**: if Phase 18 finds photon loss erodes anticoncentration (and thus hardness), this framework predicts trainability should correspondingly improve at higher loss. A candidate "what this does/doesn't establish" framing device for Phase 20's write-up, not itself a result about this project's photonic/CV realization (their treatment is qubit-based and IQP-empirical, not a proven IQP theorem).

**Correction (2026-08-19, Phase 20 review) — SUPERSEDED, see below:** Phase 18 measured the opposite of this speculative guess — anticoncentration `alpha(eta)` **increases** as loss increases, not decreases. Under Herbst et al.'s own framework this means training should, if anything, get *worse* (not better) as loss increases.

**Correction to the correction (2026-08-20, systematic verification pass):** the 2026-08-19 note above is itself withdrawn. Phase 18's reported `alpha(eta)` was computed on the raw, un-renormalized lossy distribution, so it decayed as *exactly* `eta^(2n)` — a pure survival-probability artifact, not a shape measurement (33 of 56 shipped rows reported `alpha < 1.0`, below BMS's hard theoretical floor of 1.0). Conditioned on detection, this circuit family's anticoncentration is **exactly invariant** under uniform photon loss — verified constant to 10 decimal places across the full eta grid, for both generator scopes — because uniform per-mode loss preserves the surviving distribution's shape exactly.

So the correct status of this bullet's original speculative prediction is neither "confirmed" nor "reversed": **loss neither erodes nor increases anticoncentration here, so this project's data does not test Herbst et al.'s prediction in the direction this bullet anticipated.** Both the original prediction and the 2026-08-19 correction are left on record per this project's additive-correction convention. See `docs/hardness-under-loss-study.md`'s HARD-05 section for the mechanism and evidence.
- **For Phase 18 (Hardness-Under-Loss): a real theorem exists, but the noise model needs explicit translation, not assumed equivalence (Bremner, Montanaro & Shepherd, "Achieving quantum supremacy with sparse and noisy commuting quantum computations," arXiv:1610.01808v4, *Quantum* 1, 8 (2017)).** Proves (Theorem 4) that constant-rate single-qubit **depolarizing** noise plus sufficient anticoncentration (`Σp_x² ≤ α·2⁻ⁿ`) makes IQP sampling poly-time classically simulable to any fixed ℓ1 accuracy; separately proves (Theorem 5) that encoding the same circuit with a classical error-correcting code restores hardness at any depolarizing rate `ε<1`. **This noise model is not the same physical channel as photon transmission loss**: depolarizing noise randomizes a qubit that is never removed from the register; photon loss is an erasure/leakage channel in Fock space, typically manifesting as herald failure rather than a random bit-flip within the qubit subspace. Using this paper's threshold as Phase 18's comparison point requires an explicit, stated translation model (e.g. mapping loss rate `η` to an effective depolarizing rate via this project's specific heralded-CZ/CP(α) failure mechanics), not a literal equivalence, and the paper's own anticoncentration parameter `α` (not just the noise rate `ε`) is the quantity Phase 18's methodology should track alongside `η`, per its Theorem 4.

- **arXiv:2510.24137 (Park & Oh, "Matrix product state approach to lossy boson sampling and noisy IQP sampling," KAIST, 2026) is NOT Aaronson-Brod, correcting a misattribution that appeared in this project's own `18-CONTEXT.md` first draft.** Cite its **Theorem 1** (Sec. IV, Eq. 25-26; fully read during Phase 18's `/gsd:plan-phase` research, see `18-RESEARCH.md` Finding 2): for `N` single photons through an `M`-mode passive linear-optical interferometer under uniform photon transmittance `η`, the sampled output branch's Rényi entanglement entropy is bounded `S_α(ρ_A) = O(N η^{2α})`, giving an efficiently-MPS-approximable regime whenever `η = O((log N / N)^{1/(2α)})`, up to log factors, the well-known **η = Θ(1/√N)** transmission threshold. **This is an upper bound on where one specific classical simulation method (MPS) is efficient: it is NOT a complexity-theoretic hardness lower bound above that threshold**; the paper itself states this asymmetry explicitly (MPS-inapproximability does not imply hardness, only that this particular algorithm fails to give an efficient answer). Separately, the same paper's **Section V, "Noisy IQP Sampling,"** proves a *different* result under a *different* noise channel: depth-vs-noise-rate MPS-simulability for an `n`-qubit IQP circuit under per-layer qubit-level dephasing/depolarizing noise (`d = Ω(log(n)/|log(1-2p)|)`), not photon transmittance. **Despite Section V literally carrying the "IQP" label, it is not the result being cited here for this project's photon-transmittance-loss claim: Theorem 1 is the one that physically matches this project's noise channel.** `N` here is photon count; for this project's weight-1 encoding, `N = n` (one photon per qubit).

- **arXiv:1510.05245 (Aaronson & Brod, "BosonSampling with Lost Photons," Phys. Rev. A 93, 012335 (2016)) is the genuine Aaronson-Brod paper, read in full for the first time in this project on 2026-08-14 (Plan 18-01), not previously read despite `18-CONTEXT.md`'s original text attaching this name to arXiv:2510.24137 in error.** Central result (abstract, p.1, restated as Theorem 1, p.5): *"We show that, if k out of n photons are lost, then we cannot sample classically from a distribution that is 1/n^Θ(k)-close (in total variation distance) to the ideal distribution, unless a BPP^NP machine can estimate the permanents of Gaussian matrices in n^O(k) time. In particular, if k is constant, this implies that simulating lossy BosonSampling is hard for a classical computer, under exactly the same complexity assumption used for the original lossless case."* The paper's noise model is a **fixed, constant count `k`** of photons lost from an `(n+k)`-photon experiment, a structurally different kind of quantity than a fractional rate. It states its own model explicitly (p.4): *"we are supposing that exactly k photons have been lost in the experiment, but the more physically realistic model is one where each photon has an independent probability p of being lost... if we start with N total photons, and each has probability p of being lost, then the average number of lost photons is k = pN... This shows that the model considered here can be simulated (with only polynomial overhead) by the realistic one if we set p = k/N—and hence, that a hardness result for our model carries over to the realistic one."* Loss is assumed to occur only at the input/source, not mid-circuit or at detectors, a simplification the authors flag themselves and leave as an open problem for future work (p.4, p.9). **Critically, the paper is explicit that its strong hardness guarantee degrades sharply once `k` scales with `n`**: a *constant fraction* lost (`k = εn`, this project's own regime whenever `η` is held fixed as `n` grows) only yields a guarantee to within `1/n^Θ(εn)` TVD error, which the authors state "does not allow us to make any strong complexity claims" (p.9); and for large enough `k`, "it is easy to see... that [BosonSampling becomes classically simulable] if all but O(log n) photons get lost" (p.9). **This project's own fractional-rate `η` loss model (a fixed per-mode survival probability applied uniformly, giving a random — not exactly-fixed — number of surviving photons per shot) is not directly substitutable for Aaronson-Brod's fixed count `k` without an explicit translation; for any `η` held constant as `n` grows, this project's tested loss lands in the weak/constant-fraction regime the paper's own discussion (not this project's conjecture) singles out as insufficient for a strong hardness claim.** This is exactly why HARD-04 (Plan 18-08) treats "which regime does this project's tested loss actually sit in" as an open positioning question rather than an assumed answer.

**arXiv:2510.24137 (Park & Oh) and arXiv:1510.05245 (Aaronson-Brod) are two distinct papers with two distinct noise models, cited separately in this document and in every downstream write-up (Plan 18-08, Phase 20): never merged under one name or one citation.**

## Why This Matters for Phase 9

Phase 9's photonic (Fock-space) encoding design has two qubit-side properties to keep in view as targets, not just one. The structural-hardness argument above depends on a specific circuit skeleton (commuting diagonal gates conjugated by a fixed basis change), so preserving (or knowingly trading off) that skeleton in a photonic mapping is what would keep the hardness claim intact. Separately, and only weakly correlated with structure, trainability in the qubit case turned out to hinge on initialization and system size, with structure acting as a narrow escape hatch rather than a guarantee either way. A DV/Fock-space encoding that faithfully preserves IQP's commuting-gate structure is not thereby guaranteed to inherit favorable trainability: that would need to be checked on its own terms, not assumed from the structural analogy alone.
